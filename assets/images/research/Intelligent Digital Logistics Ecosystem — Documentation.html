Intelligent Digital Logistics Ecosystem

Project Documentation

One-line idea: A unified digital platform that connects load owners, brokers, transport managers, drivers, trucks, cargo, and customers — using AI and IoT to manage the entire transportation journey, improve driver safety, optimize routes, and identify gaps in logistics infrastructure.

1. The Problem

Logistics today involves many disconnected people and systems:

  • The broker knows the load.
  • The transport manager knows the truck.
  • The driver knows the actual journey.
  • The customer just wants a delivery status.
  • The fleet operator only sees vehicle data.

No single person or system has the complete picture. On top of that:

  • Driver safety is usually handled as a separate system from logistics planning.
  • Infrastructure demand (parking, fuel, rest stops) is hard to understand by looking at individual trips.

Our project connects all of these pieces into one system.

3. Layer 1 — Digital Logistics Platform

This is the core software layer, connecting four types of users:

🏭 Load Owner / Broker

Posts a load with details such as origin, destination, weight, cargo type, and pickup date.

🚚 Transport Manager

Sees available loads and can:

  • Bid for or accept a load
  • Select a truck and assign a driver
  • Start and monitor the trip
  • Monitor vehicle and driver status
  • Handle alerts

👨‍✈️ Driver

Receives the assigned trip and follows the journey stages: Pickup → Route → Rest → Fuel → Delivery

📦 Customer

Gets a live tracking link showing status: Load booked → Truck assigned → Pickup completed → In transit → Delivered.

Key idea: everyone looks at the same shipment, but each person sees only the information relevant to their role.

5. Driver Safety System

A cabin camera monitors the driver in real time using computer vision.

Camera → OpenCV/AI → Eye closure, Yawning, Head position → Fatigue Risk
Status Meaning
🟢 Normal Driver condition normal
🟡 Warning Fatigue indicators detected — consider taking a break
🔴 High risk High fatigue risk — rest recommended

Going further than detection

Most existing systems stop at "driver is drowsy." Ours goes one step further:

Fatigue detected → Check GPS → Check route → Find nearby facilities
→ Check distance/suitability → Recommend action

Example output: "🅿️ Truck parking/rest facility 9 km ahead."

This is the core philosophy of the project: Detect → Decide → Assist.

6. AI Logistics Assistant

During the trip, the AI continuously evaluates the situation and can proactively suggest:

  • ⛽ Fuel — "Fuel level is low. Suitable fuel station 12 km ahead."
  • 🅿️ Parking — "Recommended truck parking 8 km ahead."
  • 🔧 Service — "Vehicle service facility available 15 km ahead."
  • 😴 Rest — "Driver fatigue indicators detected. Rest recommended."
  • 🚨 Emergency — "SOS received. Emergency location shared with fleet manager."

7. Smart Logistics Route

A normal navigation app asks: "What is the fastest route?" Our system asks: "What is the most suitable route for this truck, driver, and shipment?"

The route engine factors in: Traffic • Truck restrictions • Truck capacity/type • Fuel • Driver condition • Rest requirements • Parking • Service facilities • Delivery deadline

→ Output: a logistics-aware route, not just the shortest one.

8. Cargo Monitoring

Once the truck is on the road, the system watches for:

Event Trigger
Route deviation Truck leaves the expected route → Alert
Cargo door opens Unexpected door opening → Alert
Fuel anomaly Unexpected fuel decrease → Flag for manager verification

Important: the system flags anomalies for human verification — it does not automatically label every anomaly as theft.

10. Layer 3 — Infrastructure Intelligence

This is what connects the project to the smart city / infrastructure planning theme.

Instead of monitoring one truck at a time, the system aggregates data across many trips: truck movement, driver rest requests, parking searches, fuel stops, service requests, delivery density, and emergency events.

Example insight

For a corridor with 5,000 trucks passing through:

Metric Observation
Truck traffic Very High
Parking availability Low
Rest demand High
Service facilities Low
Emergency access Low

→ The system flags this as a 🔴 Logistics Infrastructure Pressure Zone, suggesting a possible need for more truck parking, rest facilities, service centres, fuel infrastructure, loading/unloading areas, or emergency support.

Scope — what this project does not try to solve

The project deliberately stays focused on logistics-related infrastructure, not general smart-city problems:

❌ Water management · ❌ Electricity distribution · ❌ Garbage collection

Instead, it focuses on:

  • Truck parking (high demand + low supply)
  • Loading zones (high delivery activity + insufficient areas)
  • Fuel/service infrastructure (heavy freight + insufficient support)
  • Emergency access (high freight movement + long distance to emergency facilities)
  • Distribution centres (high delivery concentration → possible need for local hubs)

"We are not building another GPS tracker or another load marketplace."

Our platform connects them into one digital logistics journey, and uses the combined data to deliver:

AI Assistance + Driver Safety + Infrastructure Intelligence

15. The 30-Second Pitch

"Our idea is to digitize the complete logistics journey. A broker or shipper posts a load, a transport manager selects and assigns a suitable truck and driver, and once the cargo is picked up, our IoT device continuously sends the truck's location and vehicle information to the platform. The driver gets AI-based route, rest, parking, fuel and service recommendations, while the manager and customer get real-time shipment information. We also monitor driver fatigue, vehicle anomalies, cargo events and emergencies. Finally, when data from many trips is aggregated, we can identify areas where truck parking, rest facilities, service centres or other logistics resources are insufficient. So the system doesn't just manage one truck — it creates intelligence about the entire logistics network."
